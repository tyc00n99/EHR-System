Product

Everything about a person, on one record.

Six tabs. No separate systems for the plan, the notes and the pills.

Notes

Every note the agency has written, filtered by client, caregiver, service or date. A row opens the finished note.

  • Unsigned, returned and manual notes in one filter
  • Export the pay period as CSV or PDF
  • Supervisors return a note with a reason; the caregiver fixes it

Schedule

Clients or team down the side, days across. Repeat by weekday, cancel with a reason, and see who has no one assigned.

  • Booking checks assignment and orientation first
  • A completed event opens its note in place
  • Missed events surface in the review queue

Programming

Outcomes written as the support plan states them, with a dated progress log built from the notes that addressed them.

  • Outcome, supports and how progress is measured
  • Periodic reviews: on track, needs attention, met
  • Optional daily questions when a goal wants them

Medication

A paper MAR, one week at a time. Each dose is a letter and the initials of whoever recorded it.

  • Given, refused, held or missed, with a note
  • Dose or time changes keep the history intact
  • Monthly totals on every row

Documents

A checklist of what a 245D client file must hold, and a tick when it is there. Renewals turn amber before they lapse.

  • The agency sets its own required list and cadence
  • Uploaded paper is read so it can be searched
  • Archive, never delete

Personnel files

The thirteen items a licensor asks for, with what is due in each of the next twelve months.

  • Background study, orientation, training, evaluations
  • A certificate fills in its own dates when uploaded
  • Overdue items show on the roster, not in a report
For caregivers

Clock in. Do the visit. Sign. Done.

The phone is the time clock. The note is a few fields, not a form.

1

Clock in

Only for assigned, oriented clients. The nearest scheduled event links itself.

2

Write the note

Level of assistance, supports, activities and the outcomes this visit addressed. A short review of progress.

3

Sign together

The client enters their code, the caregiver signs, and the note is accepted. Location and time are recorded for EVV.

The note

Prints the way a reviewer reads it.

Client, date, service code, hours and units up top. Narrative, supports and outcomes in the body. Both signatures at the foot. One page per visit, and a biweekly service summary for the pay period.

  • Daily service note, one per visit
  • Biweekly summary with totals per caregiver and service
  • PDF for any client, date range or pay period
