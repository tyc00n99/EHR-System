Questions agencies actually ask

We run on paper and spreadsheets today. Why change?

Because the licensor does not read your spreadsheet, they read the record. On paper, the week before a review means rebuilding notes, chasing signatures and hunting training certificates. In EVVora the record is kept the moment care happens: the note writes itself during the visit, both signatures are collected on the spot, and the personnel file tells you what is missing months before anyone asks for it.

Is EVVora actually built for 245D, or adapted from some other state's software?

Built for 245D from the first line of code. Clients are identified by PMI, units are 15 minutes, service codes and modifiers come straight from the DHS list, outcomes follow the coordinated service and support plan, and the personnel file tracks the thirteen items a Minnesota licensor checks, in the licensor's own order. Nothing had to be bent into shape, because it never fit another shape first.

How does the EVV part work?

The caregiver clocks in and out on their phone, and EVVora records GPS at both ends and checks it against the address on file. Every visit is checked against the state's EVV rules as it happens, not at month end, and visits are shaped for the HHAeXchange aggregator. Submission turns on once your agency's aggregator connection is set up, and until then nothing is sent without you knowing.

Can the office sign a note for a client or a caregiver?

No, and that is deliberate. The client signs with a code only they know, and the caregiver signs their own note. If a reviewer ever asks whether your signatures are real, you want a system where faking one is impossible, not just discouraged. Until both people have signed, the note is marked unsigned and the hours it carries are held out of pay.

What happens when a visit can't be verified, like a dead phone mid-shift?

An administrator or supervisor, never the caregiver who worked the visit, enters the whole note on one page with a required reason for why it was manual. The visit is flagged as a manual entry, it appears in your review queue, and the caregiver and client still sign it themselves afterward. Nothing is hidden, which is exactly what a county reviewer wants to see.

How do claims actually reach MNITS?

Every signed note already carries its claim line: the service code, modifiers and units, checked against the authorization before anything leaves. From there you choose. Send claims electronically through Waystar at $0.75 per clean claim line, or export and bill manually at no charge. Remittances come back onto the claim either way, so you always know what is paid, pending or denied.

Does it handle payroll?

It prepares payroll rather than running it. Each caregiver's regular and overtime hours are added up per pay period using your overtime rules and their pay rate history, ready to type into Gusto, QuickBooks or ADP in one sitting. Notes that are not fully signed are held back, so you never pay for a visit the record cannot support.

What do caregivers need to learn?

If they can use a phone browser, they can use EVVora. They sign in, tap clock in at the visit, write what happened, mark the medications, and hand the phone to the client to sign. There is no app store download and nothing to install, which matters when staff turn over.

How long does it take to get started?

Days, not months, and the implementation fee is zero. Add your staff, add your clients, and start clocking in. Intake is fast because EVVora reads the referral packet and the county's service agreement letter and fills the forms in for you to confirm. We stay hands on until your first clean pay period.

How is our data protected, and who owns it?

It is your data, full stop. Sensitive identifiers like Social Security numbers and signing codes are encrypted at rest, access follows roles you control, repeated failed sign-ins lock the account, and every change to a client, staff or visit record is logged with who made it and when. You can export your notes as PDFs and your records as spreadsheets whenever you want them.
